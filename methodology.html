Methodology & Data Provenance

What this is: per-game stat-line projections for every player on every 2026-27 regular-season game, built the same way as Dmac's NFL Projections tool — a real prior-season per-game baseline run through a chain of real-context multipliers (opponent defense, pace, home/away, rest) rather than a black-box model.

Baselines

Every returning player's starting point is their real 2025-26 per-game line (minutes, points, rebounds, assists, 3PM, steals, blocks, turnovers, FG%, FT%) sourced from Basketball-Reference team pages, fetched team-by-team to avoid truncation on the league-wide tables.

  • Returning from a season-long injury: Kyrie Irving (torn ACL), Tyrese Haliburton (torn Achilles, 2025 Finals), Damian Lillard (torn Achilles), and Fred VanVleet (torn ACL) all missed the entire 2025-26 season. Sep 2026 methodology upgrade: rather than resting on 2024-25 alone, each baseline now blends real, per-game 2023-24 and 2024-25 Basketball-Reference production, weighted by games played in each season (so the fuller-sample season counts for more) — e.g. Haliburton's 69-game 2023-24 and 73-game 2024-25 blend to roughly 49%/51%. On top of that blend, scoring volume (points, 3PM, turnovers) is nudged up or down for each player's real current-team usage context, read directly off their new teammates' real 2025-26 Usage Rate: Fred VanVleet is trimmed the most (−15%) since Houston added Kevin Durant while he was out; Damian Lillard is trimmed −13% since Portland's Shaedon Sharpe and Deni Avdija both individually out-used his own historical peak in his absence, on top of an Achilles-recovery year; Kyrie Irving is trimmed a smaller −5% for Cooper Flagg's real emergence in Dallas; Tyrese Haliburton is trimmed just −3%, since Indiana's Pascal Siakam/Nembhard/McConnell usage bump came at the expense of shots, not playmaking — and assists (which Usage Rate doesn't measure) get a small +3% bump instead, since a healthier, deeper Pacers roster gives him more to set up. Full per-player numbers and reasoning are in each player's note on the Player tab.

  • CraftedNBA individual rim-protector adjustment, Oct 8 2026: reviewed craftednba.com’s published CraftedDPM/CraftedOPM formulas (a weighted blend of DARKO, LEBRON, DRIP, BPM, and LA3 RAPM — see craftednba.com/glossary) and pulled its full ~419-player CraftedDPM leaderboard (Minutes Played and percentile filters both set to 0 to unlock the full dataset) to add a new, position-scoped matchup layer: a center now gets a small efficiency/scoring suppression (clamped ±10%/±7%) when facing an opposing team’s best currently-active individual rim protector (e.g. a Rudy Gobert, Victor Wembanyama), with part of that suppressed value redirected into assists and 3PM scaled by the player’s own existing passing/shooting profile — modeling a center settling for the pass or stepping out to the perimeter rather than forcing a contested rim shot, exactly as requested. Opponent team’s best defender is matched by player name against this tool’s own audited roster/position data, not CraftedNBA’s own team tags (found to be stale for a few players, e.g. Ivica Zubac tagged to Indiana there), and currently-"Out" players are excluded from being a team’s representative rim protector (Utah correctly falls back from an injured Jusuf Nurkic to Jaxson Hayes, for example). Non-centers are unaffected. This is scoped to CraftedDPM’s individual-defender signal specifically — the broader CraftedOPM (offensive) metric was reviewed for completeness but isn’t separately re-applied here, since this tool’s own real per-player ast/3PM rates already drive the redistribution side of the formula. Like other injury-dependent judgments in this tool, the underlying defender snapshot is static until manually refreshed — see computeProjection()’s craftedRimAdjust() for the exact formula.
  • CraftedNBA matchup adjustment generalized to all 5 positions, Oct 8 2026: the above was originally centers-only; per follow-up feedback ("when a SG is facing a great defending guard and it’s the no.1 matchup, things like that need to be taken into account") it now covers PG/SG/SF/C using CraftedNBA’s own four defender buckets (their leaderboard has no separate PF group, so a PF here is routed to the SF bucket if he shoots a real number of 3s, 3PM/g ≥ 1.0, or the C bucket if he’s more of a traditional/roll big — see craftedBucketForPlayer()). Perimeter matchups (PG/SG/SF) are modeled differently from the center case: there’s no "step out and space the floor" redirect for a guard who’s already a perimeter player, so it’s a straight efficiency/scoring/3PM suppression plus a turnover bump (ball pressure/deflections) and a modest assist suppression that’s largest for point guards specifically. New this pass: a usage-rate-based "primacy" factor (CRAFTED_PRIMACY) scales the whole effect down for anyone who isn’t their own team’s highest-usage player at that spot — a defense’s best perimeter stopper realistically gets assigned to the other team’s actual #1 option there far more consistently than to a complementary starter or bench piece, so a team’s clear #1 at a spot gets the full effect, the next-highest usage player gets about half, and everyone else gets a small residual effect. This same generalization also quietly fixed a real position-tag mismatch already in this tool’s data: Phoenix’s Oso Ighodaro is tagged PF here but plays true center (CraftedNBA itself tags him C) — the PF routing rule above correctly treats him as a center for matchup purposes, which also means he (not Khaman Maluach) is now recognized as Phoenix’s best individual rim-protection matchup threat. Verified against all 34,320 player-game projections after the change: zero errors, zero NaNs. See craftedMatchupAdjust() and craftedBucketForPlayer() for the exact formula and position-routing rules.
  • Early 2026-27 season Defense-vs-Position blend, Oct 27 2026: neither ESPN.com nor NBA.com publishes a 5-position (PG/SG/SF/PF/C) defense-vs-position breakdown at all — that's purely a third-party DFS construct, confirmed by checking both sites directly.
