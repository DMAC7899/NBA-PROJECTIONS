Methodology & Data Provenance

What this is: per-game stat-line projections for every player on every 2026-27 regular-season game, built the same way as Dmac's NFL Projections tool — a real prior-season per-game baseline run through a chain of real-context multipliers (opponent defense, pace, home/away, rest) rather than a black-box model.

Baselines

Every returning player's starting point is their real 2025-26 per-game line (minutes, points, rebounds, assists, 3PM, steals, blocks, turnovers, FG%, FT%) sourced from Basketball-Reference team pages, fetched team-by-team to avoid truncation on the league-wide tables.

  • CraftedNBA matchup adjustment generalized to all 5 positions, Oct 8 2026: the above was originally centers-only; per follow-up feedback ("when a SG is facing a great defending guard and it’s the no.1 matchup, things like that need to be taken into account") it now covers PG/SG/SF/C using CraftedNBA’s own four defender buckets (their leaderboard has no separate PF group, so a PF here is routed to the SF bucket if he shoots a real number of 3s, 3PM/g ≥ 1.0, or the C bucket if he’s more of a traditional/roll big — see craftedBucketForPlayer()). Perimeter matchups (PG/SG/SF) are modeled differently from the center case: there’s no "step out and space the floor" redirect for a guard who’s already a perimeter player, so it’s a straight efficiency/scoring/3PM suppression plus a turnover bump (ball pressure/deflections) and a modest assist suppression that’s largest for point guards specifically. New this pass: a usage-rate-based "primacy" factor (CRAFTED_PRIMACY) scales the whole effect down for anyone who isn’t their own team’s highest-usage player at that spot — a defense’s best perimeter stopper realistically gets assigned to the other team’s actual #1 option there far more consistently than to a complementary starter or bench piece, so a team’s clear #1 at a spot gets the full effect, the next-highest usage player gets about half, and everyone else gets a small residual effect. This same generalization also quietly fixed a real position-tag mismatch already in this tool’s data: Phoenix’s Oso Ighodaro is tagged PF here but plays true center (CraftedNBA itself tags him C) — the PF routing rule above correctly treats him as a center for matchup purposes, which also means he (not Khaman Maluach) is now recognized as Phoenix’s best individual rim-protection matchup threat. Verified against all 34,320 player-game projections after the change: zero errors, zero NaNs. See craftedMatchupAdjust() and craftedBucketForPlayer() for the exact formula and position-routing rules.

Matchup adjustments

Each projection multiplies the baseline by:

  • Defense vs. Position (DvP) — real per-position defensive splits for PTS, REB, AST, 3PM, STL, BLK, and TOV, originally pulled from BettingPros' Defense vs. Position cheat sheet (2025-26 season, all 30 teams × all 5 positions), cross-checked and recalibrated against dunkest.com's Defense vs Position tool on Oct 2, 2026 — see the recalibration note below. FG% and FT% aren't published by either source, so those two stats keep their prior hashtagbasketball.com per-position splits, untouched by the recalibration. For each stat, this is what the opponent has actually allowed to a player's specific position (PG/SG/SF/PF/C) this season, compared to the league-average allowed to that position. This is the primary matchup signal — a team that's unusually generous to point guards specifically will show up here even if its overall Defensive Rating is average. It's blended 70/30 with the team's overall Defensive Rating (below) to smooth over small-sample noise in any single position split. The "Opp Rk vPos" column shown in the Player Game Log and Slate Finder tables is this same DvP data expressed as a 1–30 league rank by PTS allowed to that position — 1st is the stingiest defense at that spot, 30th is the most generous. Red = ranked 1st–10th (tough matchup), gray = 11th–20th (average), green = 21st–30th (favorable matchup).
  • Pace tempo — each opponent's real 2025-26 pace (possessions per 48 minutes) is also shown as a FAST / AVG / SLOW tag next to the exact number: FAST is a top-10 pace team (green — more possessions typically means more counting-stat opportunity), SLOW is a bottom-10 pace team (red — fewer possessions), AVG is the middle 10. This is the same real pace figure already baked into the projection math (see Pace below) — the tag is just a faster visual read of it.
  • Opponent defense (overall) — opponent's real 2025-26 Defensive Rating vs. the league average (${fmt(LEAGUE_AVG.drtg,1)}), used as the 30% blend partner to DvP above rather than standing alone.
  • Pace — the average of both teams' real 2025-26 pace vs. league average (${fmt(LEAGUE_AVG.pace,1)}) scales all counting stats up or down with the expected number of possessions.
  • Own-team offense (added Oct 7, 2026): the player's own team's real 2025-26 Offensive Rating vs. the league average now acts as a modest additional multiplier on points, assists, and 3PM specifically — shown as an "Offense" badge on the Player tab and as part of the Slate Finder and By-Date matchup lines. It's intentionally damped to about half the strength of the opponent-defense multiplier above and clamped to a tighter ±8%/+10% band, rather than the ±16%/+20% used for opponent defense, because a player's baseline is already their own real production from playing in that team's offensive system — applying the full-strength version a second time would double-count the same signal for anyone still on their original team. Its main value is for the 100+ players who changed teams this offseason, whose baseline production was generated on a different team's offense than the one they're now projected on; it also captures a small further pace/shot-quality effect on top of the raw baseline for everyone else. Not applied to rebounds, steals, blocks, turnovers, or shooting percentages, which are driven more by individual role and the opponent matchup than by the player's own scoring environment.
  • Home/away — a modest split (+2% home / -2% away) reflecting the real home-court scoring edge.
  • Rest & back-to-backs — computed directly from the real dated 2026-27 schedule for every team. The second night of a back-to-back knocks down minutes and production; 3+ days of rest gives a small bump.
  • Shooting percentages (FG%/FT%) use the same DvP/DRtg blend but damped heavily versus counting stats — a matchup shifts volume far more than a player's actual shooting touch. Free throw shooting is treated as matchup-independent.

Oct 2, 2026 update: Dunkest cross-check & recalibration, 2-year DvP trend, coaching scheme carryover

Prompted by a user request to verify the DvP table against dunkest.com's Defense vs Position tool and extend it with multi-year trend and coaching context, three things were built:

  • Cross-check found the existing DvP table was running systematically high. All 42 relevant dunkest.com pages (2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons × 7 stat categories × its 3 positional groups — Guards/Forwards/Centers, each filtered by dunkest to players with at least 28 minutes played) were pulled live via browser automation and compared against the existing BettingPros-sourced table, aggregating this app's PG+SG into dunkest's "Guards" and SF+PF into its "Forwards" for an apples-to-apples check. The BettingPros-sourced numbers ran 23-65% higher than dunkest's across every single stat and position group, with no team-specific pattern — a uniform, directional bias (worst on rebounds and blocks allowed to centers, e.g. the league-wide "PTS allowed to Centers" average was 22.1 in the old table vs. 16.6 on dunkest). That consistent, one-directional gap — rather than team-by-team noise — points to a methodology difference in how the original BettingPros pull counted qualifying minutes, not a one-off scraping error, so the existing table has been recalibrated: for each team/stat, a scale factor (dunkest's verified Guards/Forwards/Centers number ÷ the existing PG+SG or SF+PF average) was computed and applied to each individual PG/SG/SF/PF/C value, preserving whatever real split this app's data already showed between e.g. PG and SG while correcting the overall level to match the verified source. Centers (a 1:1 group match, no aggregation needed) were corrected the same way. FG%/FT% are untouched (dunkest doesn't publish them). Scale factors ranged 0.37–1.04 (median 0.73). The DvP grid, rank badges, and "Opp Rk vPos" column throughout the app now reflect these recalibrated numbers.
  • 2-Year DvP Trend panel (bottom of the Defensive Profile tab) shows, at dunkest's native Guards/Forwards/Centers grain, how each team's defense at each spot moved from 2024-25 to 2025-26 for all 7 stat categories — directly answering the "pull data from 2024 to now and see how teams are trending" part of the request. This trend data is shown separately from (not blended into) the main recalibrated PG/SG/SF/PF/C table above, since dunkest's 3-group split is coarser than this app's 5-position grid and blending them would have thrown away real resolution between, say, point guards and shooting guards.
  • Head coach & defensive scheme carryover (also on the Defensive Profile tab, per team): current 2026-27 head coach and tenure for all 30 teams, researched live via web search on Oct 2, 2026. Six teams hired a new head coach this offseason (Bulls: Tiago Splitter; Mavericks: Dusty May; Magic: Sean Sweeney; Bucks: Taylor Jenkins; Pelicans: Jamahl Mosley; Trail Blazers: Micah Nori). For the three with a well-documented, multi-year prior-team defensive track record — Jamahl Mosley (Orlando → New Orleans; built top-5 defenses around Orlando's length for 5 seasons), Taylor Jenkins (Memphis → Milwaukee, returning to the organization where he once was a Budenholzer-era assistant; built an aggressive, turnover-forcing Memphis defense for 6 seasons), and Micah Nori (Minnesota lead assistant → Portland head coach; co-architect of the Gobert-anchored defense that finished top-3 in 2025-26) — a small, tightly-clamped numeric nudge (0.5-1.5%, see COACH_ADJ in the source) is applied to that team's overall defensive multiplier, heavily regressed since the new team's actual personnel differs from where that track record was built. The other three new hires get no numeric adjustment: Sean Sweeney (Orlando) spent his most recent stop as a Spurs associate head coach under a Wembanyama-shaped system Orlando doesn't have the personnel to replicate, and inherits a roster whose elite defensive identity was built on personnel as much as scheme — treated as a wash; Dusty May (Dallas) is a college hire with zero NBA coaching data to extrapolate from; Tiago Splitter (Chicago) was Portland's interim coach for half a season, too short a sample to characterize. This is intentionally conservative — real DRtg data (which already drives 30% of every matchup projection) is left completely untouched; the coaching nudge is a separate, clearly-labeled, order-of-magnitude-smaller layer on top of it, not a replacement for real results once the 2026-27 season actually tips off.

Defensive Profile tab

The Defensive Profile tab is the full "what does this defense give up, and to whom" view: pick a team and see, for every position (PG/SG/SF/PF/C), the real 2025-26 DvP number for every stat category (PTS, REB, AST, 3PM, STL, BLK, TOV, FG%, FT%) plus its 1–30 league rank at that specific stat/position — the same rank convention as "Opp Rk vPos" elsewhere (1 = stingiest, 30 = most generous). Below the grid, "Biggest Weaknesses" surfaces every position/stat combo where that team ranks 24th–30th league-wide (the clearest matchups to attack), and "Biggest Strengths" surfaces every combo ranked 1st–7th (the matchups to avoid). Below that, a Head Coach & Defensive Scheme card and a 2-Year DvP Trend table — see the Oct 2, 2026 update above for both.

Slate Finder: Games By Date & History vs Opponent

Slate Finder is now split into three sub-tabs. Matchup is the original per-team, per-game view. Games By Date lists every real 2026-27 matchup on a selected date league-wide, with each team's single most-generous position/stat combo surfaced as a quick "who do they give up the most to" read, plus a one-click link into that team's full Matchup slate. History vs Opponent picks a player and an opponent they face multiple times this season (division rivals typically meet 4x, others 2–3x) and lines up every 2026-27 meeting against that specific opponent side by side. This is not a real historical box-score log — the source data only has 2025-26 season averages, not real per-game 2025-26 results, so no genuine prior head-to-head log exists to pull from. It uses the same matchup-adjusted projection engine as the rest of the app for each scheduled meeting, which makes repeat-opponent games easy to compare, but it's not "what actually happened last time these two played."

Usage Rate (USG%)

The Team Roster and Player tabs show each player's real 2025-26 Usage Rate — the standard NBA advanced stat measuring what share of their team's possessions a player used (via a shot attempt, free throws, or turnover) while on the floor — pulled directly from Basketball-Reference's league-wide Advanced stats table. 428 of 439 players have this real, sourced figure. The remaining 11 — the four season-long-injury returners (Kyrie Irving, Tyrese Haliburton, Damian Lillard, Fred VanVleet) and six 2026 draft rookies plus Yaxel Lendeborg, none of whom have 2025-26 NBA minutes to pull a real figure from — get an estimated USG%, flagged with a "*" and an orange badge, from a linear regression fit on the 429 real values (R² = 0.85) using each player's own real per-game PTS/AST/TOV/MPG as inputs.

Teammate OUT — usage redistribution simulator

Both the Player Game Log and Slate Finder tabs let you simulate a specific teammate being OUT and see how that player's projection changes. The model: each remaining rostered teammate is given a weight equal to their own real Usage Rate (a proxy for how much offense already runs through them), boosted 30% for teammates who share the OUT player's primary position (who typically inherit the specific vacated role, not just generic extra touches). Weights are normalized into a "share" of the departed player's production for each remaining teammate. That share is applied as: up to 60% of the OUT player's minutes (capped at 40 total), 75% of their points/rebounds/3PM/steals/blocks/turnovers scaled by share (damped further per stat — e.g. rebounds and steals/blocks absorb less than points), and assists scaled higher (1.15x) for a same-position teammate or lower (0.85x) otherwise, since ball-handling duties transfer more directly to a like-positioned player. This is a heuristic estimate, not a guarantee — real NBA usage redistribution after an injury or rest day depends on the coach's actual game plan, who else is on the floor, and matchup-specific decisions that no formula fully captures. Use it as a directional "who benefits most" read, not a locked-in projection.

"Line" and "Edge" — read this before using it for betting

Sportsbooks do not publish individual player prop lines for games months in advance — those are set the day of (or a day or two before) each game. So the "Line" shown for each stat is not a scraped live sportsbook numbers — it's the player's own real 2025-26 season baseline, rounded to the nearest half-point, used as a stand-in for "the number this player would typically be lined at." The "Edge" column is simply the matchup-adjusted projection minus that baseline. A positive edge means the matchup (opponent defense, pace, rest, home/away) pushes the player meaningfully above their normal production; a negative edge means the opposite. Use it as a directional signal for which matchups to research further against the actual line posted closer to game time — not as the line itself.

Known gaps

  • DvP splits are current-season (2025-26) snapshots — PTS/REB/AST/3PM/STL/BLK/TOV from BettingPros (recalibrated against dunkest.com, see the Oct 2, 2026 update above), FG%/FT% from hashtagbasketball.com — not adjusted for 2026-27 roster turnover on the defensive side — a team that overhauled its perimeter defense this offseason won't reflect that until real 2026-27 data is available. Combo-listed positions (e.g. a player listed as "PG-SG") use only their primary position for the DvP lookup. The recalibration corrects the overall level using dunkest's coarser Guards/Forwards/Centers grouping; it can't independently verify the PG-vs-SG or SF-vs-PF split within each group since dunkest doesn't publish at that grain.
  • The coaching scheme carryover nudge (Head Coach panel, Defensive Profile tab) is qualitative research turned into a deliberately tiny numeric input — it is not based on any 2026-27 game having actually been played, and a coach's defensive identity depends heavily on personnel fit that can't be known until the season starts. Treat it as a thumb on the scale, not a forecast.
  • Depth-chart/role changes for returning players (a bench player inheriting a bigger role because a teammate left, a starter getting squeezed by a new addition, etc.) are not individually modeled — the baseline assumes each returning player's role holds roughly steady at their real 2025-26 usage level. This is separate from the team reassignments and rookie/injury-return handling described above, which are modeled.
  • A current injury-status badge (Out/Questionable/Day-To-Day/Recovering) is now shown where a designation was found, refreshed most recently as of Oct 7, 2026 — see the methodology note above. It does not change the projection math itself (a "Questionable" player's baseline still assumes their normal usage level); it's a visual flag, not an automatic adjustment. Use the "Teammate OUT" simulator if you want to see projections recalculated around a specific player being out. Coverage isn't exhaustive — a player with no badge simply had no current designation found in the sources checked, not a guarantee of full health, and the snapshot goes stale as camp/preseason news breaks.
  • Team pace/ORtg/DRtg figures are full-season 2025-26 averages, not adjusted for any 2026-27 roster turnover on that side of the ball.
  • The own-team offense multiplier (added Oct 7, 2026, see above) is deliberately damped for exactly this reason: for a player still on the same team whose stats were recorded on, that team's real ORtg already partially describes the environment their own baseline came from, so applying it again is a soft double-count by design, not an oversight — the damping and tight clamp are there to keep that double-count small. It carries real, non-duplicated signal mainly for traded/signed players whose baseline production happened on a different team's offense.
  • "History vs Opponent" shows projected, not actual, results for each scheduled meeting — see above. Usage Rate is a real 2025-26 figure for 428 of 439 players; the rest are regression-estimated (flagged with *). The "Teammate OUT" simulator is a heuristic redistribution model, not a play-by-play or lineup-data-driven projection — it doesn't know about a specific replacement starter, a coach's stated rotation plans, or anything else that happened after this data was pulled.
